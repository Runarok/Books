### **66. Dying Before Death**

*Ego death and transformation*

There are moments in life when the self we know — the identity, roles, and assumptions we cling to — quietly dissolves. This is ego death, a subtle form of dying before physical death, where old beliefs, habits, or attachments no longer serve and fall away. It can feel destabilizing, disorienting, or even frightening, but it is also a doorway to transformation, offering space for a new sense of self to emerge.

Ego death does not happen all at once. It is often gradual, emerging through crisis, reflection, or intense experience. It can feel like losing parts of who you were, a dismantling of comfort and control. Yet this unmaking is necessary for growth — without shedding outdated frameworks, we cannot create new ways of thinking, relating, or being.

The process teaches humility and perspective. As old identities fade, attachment to outcomes, expectations, or illusions of permanence loosens. Life becomes less about defending a fixed self and more about engaging with reality openly, adapting, and embracing change. The ego’s dissolution is not erasure but refinement — clearing space for deeper awareness, empathy, and authenticity.

Dying before death, then, is a practice of transformation. It reminds us that identity is fluid, that change is inevitable, and that growth often requires letting go. By confronting the temporary nature of the self we hold, we discover resilience, flexibility, and the capacity to step into life renewed, aware that each ending within can become the beginning of something vital and enduring.
