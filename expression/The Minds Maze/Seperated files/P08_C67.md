
### **67. The Fragility of Flesh**

*Being mortal in a digital age*

Despite the illusions of permanence that technology and media offer, the body remains finite, vulnerable, and subject to decay. The fragility of flesh is a constant reminder that life is limited, that no matter how fast information travels or how long memories persist online, our physical selves have boundaries. Mortality is unavoidable, and it shapes how we experience every day, every relationship, and every achievement.

In the digital age, this fragility becomes paradoxically more visible yet more ignored. We see avatars, social media profiles, and curated lives that appear timeless, but behind the screens, flesh endures fatigue, injury, and decline. This tension between virtual continuity and bodily impermanence creates both distraction and denial, masking the urgency that mortality naturally imposes.

Awareness of the body’s limits encourages presence. Recognizing fragility reminds us to value time, to honor health, and to engage fully with experiences rather than postpone living for the future. Mortality, far from being a morbid thought, sharpens perception, prioritizes meaning, and fosters appreciation for the tangible, fleeting moments of life.

Ultimately, being mortal in a digital age is a lesson in balance. It challenges us to navigate the allure of endless virtual permanence while honoring the finite reality of our bodies. It teaches humility, care, and the necessity of grounding ourselves in experiences that matter — in touch, presence, and the simple yet profound truths of being alive.
