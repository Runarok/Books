### **30. The First Wound**

*How attachment scripts begin*

The earliest relationships leave marks that last far longer than childhood itself. The first wound is rarely dramatic; it can be a small moment of neglect, a harsh word, or the absence of comfort when it was needed. Yet these experiences quietly shape how you learn to connect, trust, and seek love. They form the scripts that guide attachment, often unconsciously, influencing how you approach relationships throughout life.

These early wounds are not always obvious. They can appear as patterns of fear, avoidance, or dependence — the invisible rules you follow when relating to others. You might notice a hesitation to trust, an urge to please, or a fear of being abandoned. Each is a reflection of lessons learned long ago, encoded in emotional memory before you could even articulate them.

Understanding the first wound is not about blaming the past or those who shaped it. It’s about noticing the origin of your relational patterns, recognizing the scripts you inherited, and beginning to question whether they still serve you. Awareness allows you to respond differently, to form attachments with intention rather than habit, and to engage in connection that is more conscious and authentic.

The first wound is both a scar and a teacher. It reveals the beginnings of how you love and are loved, the roots of fear and desire, and the foundation upon which all future relationships are built. By acknowledging it, you gain insight into the invisible forces that have guided your heart, offering a chance to rewrite your relational story with awareness and care.
