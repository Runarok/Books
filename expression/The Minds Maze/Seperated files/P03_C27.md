### **27. Seasons of the Self**

*How identity evolves cyclically*

Just like nature, the self moves in cycles. There are periods of growth, flowering, and expansion, followed by times of quiet, reflection, and shedding. These seasons of the self are not always obvious, yet they shape the rhythm of our lives, the choices we make, and the way we see ourselves in the world.

During “spring” moments, curiosity and ambition push you outward. You experiment, learn, and take risks, building new skills and exploring possibilities. “Summer” brings full expression: confidence, energy, and visibility, where your actions and presence bloom in the world. “Autumn” signals reflection, harvesting lessons from experience, letting go of what no longer serves, and preparing for the coming quiet. “Winter” is inward, introspective, and restorative — a time of pause, solitude, and integration.

These cycles are natural and inevitable. Resisting them creates tension, frustration, or burnout, while honoring them allows growth to feel organic and sustainable. Understanding the seasons of the self is about recognizing that change is not linear. Identity is not a single fixed state; it evolves in response to time, experiences, and inner rhythm.

By observing these cycles, you learn patience with yourself, awareness of patterns, and acceptance of transformation as part of life. Each season has its purpose, and each stage is necessary. The self is never static, and in embracing these cycles, you find the freedom to grow without losing touch with who you are in any given moment.
